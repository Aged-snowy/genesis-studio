GENESIS
The Book of the World, as told by the Prophet
A world by PixelProphetOG

Wanderer.

Before there was anything, there was the word. I spoke, and Genesis answered. Hills rose where I named them, rivers ran where I asked,
and the first stones settled into the shapes I described. When the world was ready, I gave its greatest gift away: to you.

Whatever you describe, Genesis will build. Speak the word /build, and then say what you wish to see, in plain words, the way you
would tell a friend. A castle with a gold roof. A cottage with a garden and two cats. A tiny castle beneath a giant glass dome. A city.

But a world is more than what stands in it. Beneath your feet lie ores I buried for you. In the wild walk creatures I dreamed. Behind
three sealed gates wait the realms I made before this one. And somewhere, scattered and torn, lie the pages of my journal, which tell
how all of this came to be, and what still threatens it.

This book holds everything I know. The Codex in your hands holds the same, page by page, for when you are in the world.

I know some 380 thousand words, 1657 blocks of the old world and 631 of my own, 1316 items and 79 creatures.
I forgive misspellings. /genesis works the same as /build.

SPEAKING A CREATION
  Speak the word /build and say what you wish to see. I listen for a form, a size, up to four materials, themes, creatures, treasure
  and touches. Whatever you leave unsaid, I choose well. A theme can choose the form, a material can choose the form, and a single
  material brings a roof and trim that suit it.
  Materials fall in this order: walls, trim and pillars, roof, then details, glass and lights.
  Or name the part yourself: gold roof, stone walls, walls made of cherry, copper trim, glass windows.

  Some things to say:
    /build a manor with two wings, a balcony and a chimney
    /build a huge spooky castle with a gold roof, a moat, 4 iron golems, and diamonds in a chest
    /build cozy cottage with a garden and 2 cats
    /build a farm with 5 cows and 3 sheep
    /build pool with 6 tropical fish and an axolotl
    /build citadel of polished granite with copper trim
    /build a huge dungeon
    /build a tiny stone castle surrounded by a giant glass dome
    /build a tower on top of a mountain
    /build a modern city
    /build a giant statue of a dragon

THINGS TOGETHER
  Speak of how two things stand, and I will honor it.
  surrounded by, encircled by, ringed by, under a: the second thing holds the first. A tiny castle surrounded by a giant glass dome
    raises the castle, then a dome sized and set so every corner of the castle fits inside.
    If the second thing is small, or many, it forms a ring instead: a glass dome surrounded by small towers, or a castle with 6 towers around it.
  inside, within, in the middle of: a garden inside a dome, a house inside a glass sphere.
  on top of, atop, upon: a tower on top of a mountain. The second thing rises first, and the first stands on its highest point.
  under, beneath, below: a crypt under a church.
  next to, beside, near: a house next to a barn.
  A creation of many parts is unmade all at once with a single /undo.

CITIES
  Say small city, city, great city or huge city, and I lay out a grid of streets with lamps at every crossing, clear the ground, and
  raise each district in turn. A great hall or cathedral stands at the heart, markets and workshops around it, homes beyond, farms and
  stables at the rim of the larger cities, and towers at the corners.
  Small cities have 9 districts, cities 16, great cities 25, and the vast ones 36.
  Add a style and every district wears it: a cherry city, a spooky city, a desert city. Say modern city for towers, stations and hospitals.
  When it stands, place a Town Charter in its heart and folk will come to live there.

SEEING BEFORE RAISING
  Say /build preview and any description. I mark where it would stand with a glowing outline, and tell you how many blocks it would take
  and of what. Say /build yes to raise it exactly as shown, or /build no to let it fade.
  The Codex can do the same: in Speak a Creation, turn on Show me its outline and cost first.
  If the World Rules say creations cost their blocks, a creation in survival takes what it needs from your inventory, and tells you
  exactly what you lack if you do not have it all.

RESHAPING THE LAST CREATION
  Once a creation stands, you may change it. I unmake the old shape and raise the new one in the same place, with the same details.
  /build bigger and /build smaller, or with a percent: /build bigger 50.
  /build turn right, /build turn left, /build turn around.
  /build mirror swaps its left and right.
  /build move left 10, or forward, back, up, down, north, south, east or west, and how far.
  /build recolor gold, or name a part: /build recolor red roof, /build recolor cherry walls.
  /build replace oak planks with cherry planks trades one block for another everywhere in it.
  The Codex has a page for all of these: Reshape the Last Creation.

YOUR GROWTH
  You carry one level and ten skills: combat, mining, building, smithing and crafting, exploring, magic, farming, cooking, engineering
  and taming. Each grows as you use it, and all of it adds to your level. Every level brings a perk point.
  Perks come in eight paths, four in each, and the later ones need the earlier:
  Warrior: Tough (Two extra hearts, always); Strong (Strength I, always); Berserker (Every kill heals you for a few seconds); Champion (Four more hearts and resistance I).
  Explorer: Swift (Speed I, always); Leaper (Jump boost I, always); Night Eyes (See in the dark, always); Treasure Sense (Better loot from chests, elites and bosses).
  Miner: Digger (Haste I, always); Lucky Vein (Ores sometimes drop double); Deep Sight (Mining an ore tells you about other ores nearby); Human Drill (Haste II, always).
  Mage: Mana Pool (50 more mana); Quick Cast (Spells cost a third less mana); Arcane Shield (Casting a spell gives you extra hearts for a moment); Archmage (100 more mana, and mana comes back twice as fast).
  Builder: Architect (Your builds go up 50 percent faster); Patron (Every build you make earns you coins); Scholar (25 percent more experience from everything); Visionary (Builds you describe come out detailed and furnished every time).
  Engineer: Tinker (Your machines run 25 percent faster); Efficient (Your machines use a third less flux); Overclock (Your generators make half again as much flux); Mastermind (Tech weapons and drills wear half as fast, and your drone fights for you).
  Hearth: Green Thumb (Your crops sometimes give double); Angler (Luck of the sea, always); Master Chef (Meals you eat last twice as long); Homesteader (Regeneration while you stand in your own town).
  Beastmaster: Beast Whisperer (Wild beasts are tamed on the first try more often); Pack Leader (Your companions are stronger and tougher); Born Rider (Mounts and griffins carry you faster); Wild Heart (Neutral beasts never turn on you, and rare beasts find you more often).

  Loot comes in five rarities: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Epic and Legendary, with powers written beneath the name.
  Some monsters are born elite, with a title and a power. They are tougher and leave better things behind.
  Daily Tasks: three each morning. They pay in gold and sometimes gear.
  The Market buys and sells with coins. Gold is worth 10, silver 5, copper 1.
  Rifts: on some nights a Rift tears open. Survive three waves and a chest appears. /build rift tells you where it is.

ORES AND METALS
  New ores appear in land you have not yet explored, so wander far or begin a new world.
  Mine the ore, smelt the raw metal into ingots, or crush it in a Crusher for two dusts. Fortune works on every ore I made.
  Tin: Common in stone from sea level to the hills. Used for bronze, solder for circuits, cans and cables.
    Soft and humble. Alone it is little. With copper it built the first age.
  Zinc: Stone at middle depths, richest beneath the red mesas. Used for brass, batteries and galvanized parts.
    A blue grey metal that guards others from rust.
  Nickel: Stone and deepslate below the surface. Used for invar, magnets, batteries and heat proof parts.
    Stubborn under the hammer and calm in the fire.
  Lead: Deepslate, heavy veins far below. Used for solder, reactor shielding, sinkers and weights.
    The heaviest of the common metals. It shields what must not escape.
  Aluminum: Bauxite near the surface of mesas, savannas and deserts. Used for duralumin, gliders, light armor and wiring.
    Light as a feather and quick to carry a current.
  Platinum: Rare and deep, below Y -24. Used for rings, amulets, spell foci and fine circuits.
    The metal of kings and mages. It never tarnishes.
  Tungsten: Wolframite under the high mountains. Used for hammers, excavators, drill bits and furnace linings.
    Nothing melts it and little scratches it.
  Titanium: Ilmenite in deepslate, scattered and shy. Used for light strong armor, daggers, spears and fast pickaxes.
    As strong as steel at half the weight.
  Cobalt: Blue veins in the netherrack of the Nether. Used for the fastest mining tools, blue dyes and magnets.
    It sings when struck. Miners swear it digs by itself.
  Uranium: Faintly glowing, at the very bottom of the world. Used for reactor fuel. keep it far from your food.
    It burns without fire. Respect it.
  Silver: Stone and deepslate from Y -16 to 56. Used for weapons that wound the undead, electrum, mirrors and coins.
    Moonlight made solid. The dead fear its touch.
  Mythril: Deep stone and deepslate, Y -48 to 12. Used for magic armor, staffs, aetherium and the swiftest blades.
    Light as silk and humming with the Weave.
  Adamantite: The deepest deepslate, below Y -24. Used for the heaviest armor and weapons of all.
    The bones of the world. Worth every swing of the pick.
  Orichalcum: Lush caves and the roots of jungles. Used for spell power, scythes and arcane robes.
    The lost metal of a drowned kingdom. It remembers spells.
  Infernium: Burning veins in the Nether, thickest near basalt and lava. Used for emberguard armor, fire weapons and soulsteel.
    Born in the fire and never cools.
  Starmetal: Only where a falling star strikes the ground, and in the Starfall Expanse. Used for starsteel, celestial armor and the star key.
    Iron that fell from the sky, still cold from the void.

ALLOYS
  Alloys are joined at the Forge, or in an Alloy Smelter. Bronze, brass and steel you know from the start. The rest come through research.
  Bronze: 3 copper ingot and 1 tin ingot make 4. The first alloy. The first age.
  Brass: 2 copper ingot and 1 zinc ingot make 3. Bright as gold and far more useful.
  Steel: 2 iron ingot and 2 coal make 2. Iron that learned discipline in the fire.
  Invar: 2 iron ingot and 1 nickel ingot make 3. It does not swell in the heat. Engineers love it.
  Electrum: 1 gold ingot and 1 silver ingot make 2. Pale gold that carries both current and charm.
  Solder: 1 tin ingot and 1 lead ingot make 2. The glue of the machine age.
  Duralumin: 3 aluminum ingot and 1 copper ingot make 4. Hard aluminum, the skin of flying machines.
  Starsteel: 1 starmetal ingot and 2 steel ingot make 2. Steel tempered with a fallen star.
  Soulsteel: 1 infernium ingot and 2 soul crystal and 1 steel ingot make 2. It whispers. Do not listen too long.
  Aetherium: 2 mythril ingot and 2 aether crystal make 2. Mythril woven with the light of the high isles.
  Voidsteel: 2 void crystal and 1 netherite scrap and 2 steel ingot make 2. Forged where nothing is. It weighs less than it should.

MAGIC
  Magic in Genesis is a grammar. Every spell joins an element to a form. Fire and bolt make a fire bolt. Life and ward make a healing
  ward. Learn a rune by using it, inscribe blank runes at the Arcane Altar, and set spells into the four slots of the Grand Grimoire.
  Cast with a staff. Mana returns over time, and faster for those who wear the arcane set or walk the Mage path.
  Elements: Fire, Frost, Storm, Earth, Life, Void, Arcane, Shadow, Air.
  Bolt: A missile that flies where you look.
  Burst: An explosion of power around you.
  Wall: A wall of the element before you.
  Ward: A shield that guards you.
  Self: The element empowers your own body.
  Bridge: A path of the element over a gap.
  Rain: The element falls from the sky where you look.
  Summon: Calls a spirit of the element to fight beside you.

  Named spells, learned through research and old pages:
  Light (6 mana): A floating light where you look, and clear sight.
  Mend (20 mana): Repairs a tenth of what you hold.
  Blink (12 mana): Step instantly where you look.
  Recall (40 mana): Return to where you sleep.
  Levitate (18 mana): Rise into the air for a few breaths.
  Veil (25 mana): Become unseen for half a minute.
  Growth (25 mana): Crops and saplings around you grow.
  Meteor (70 mana): Call a falling star down on your foes.
  Stillness (60 mana): Everything around you nearly stops.
  Call the Golem (60 mana): An iron guardian fights for you for a minute.
  Decoy (30 mana): A double of you draws your foes away.

MACHINES AND FLUX
  Flux is the lifeblood of machines. Generators make it, cables and relays carry it, batteries keep it, and machines spend it.
  Every joined group of cables and machines is one network, and flux is shared through it. A Flux Relay reaches anything within
  16 blocks without cables. Put a chest beside a machine and it takes its inputs from there and leaves its outputs.
  Use a machine to see its state, switch it on or off, and set its options. A wrench reads it at a glance. The Codex page Machines Near Me
  lists every machine around you.
  Combustion Generator: Burns coal, charcoal, blaze rods or lava. Use it with fuel in hand. Holds 4000 flux.
  Solar Panel: Makes flux under an open sky by day. Holds 2000 flux.
  Wind Turbine: The higher it stands, the more it makes. Holds 2000 flux.
  Water Wheel: Turns with water flowing beside it. Holds 2000 flux.
  Geothermal Generator: Draws heat from lava beside it. Holds 4000 flux.
  Fission Reactor: Burns uranium for great power. Keep water beside it, or it overheats. Holds 20000 flux.
  Flux Battery: Stores flux for when it is needed. Holds 60000 flux.
  Flux Cable: Carries flux to anything it touches. Holds 0 flux.
  Flux Relay: Sends flux through the air to other relays within 16 blocks. Holds 0 flux.
  Electric Furnace: Smelts what is in the chest above it into the chest below it. Holds 2000 flux.
  Crusher: Grinds ores and raw metal into twice the dust. Chest above, chest below. Holds 2000 flux.
  Alloy Smelter: Makes any alloy you know from the chest above into the chest below. Holds 2000 flux.
  Quarry: Digs a square shaft beneath it and fills the chest beside it. Holds 4000 flux.
  Harvester: Harvests and replants ripe crops around it into the chest beside it. Holds 2000 flux.
  Item Pipe: Moves items from the container behind it to the one in front. Holds 0 flux.
  Conveyor Belt: Carries items and creatures the way it faces. Holds 0 flux.
  Charging Station: Recharges drills, rifles, blasters and gliders of anyone near. Holds 4000 flux.
  Teleporter Pad: Link two pads. Step on one to arrive at the other. Holds 6000 flux.
  Elevator Platform: Jump to rise to the next platform above. Sneak to go down. Holds 0 flux.
  Ward Pylon: Keeps monsters out of a 12 block circle. Holds 4000 flux.
  Arrow Turret: Shoots monsters within 16 blocks. Holds 3000 flux.
  Pump: Drains water and lava below it. Holds 2000 flux.
  Assembler: Makes a chosen Forge or Loom recipe from the chest above into the chest below. Holds 3000 flux.

  Vehicles: a Rover Kit sets down a ground vehicle. An Airship Kit calls an airship that flies wherever you look while you ride.

SEASONS, WEATHER AND NIGHT
  Spring, summer, autumn and winter each last eight days. Crops grow best in their own season, slowly out of it, and not at all in
  winter unless they are sheltered under glass.
  Temperature, when turned on in the World Rules, warms you in deserts and the Nether and chills you in snowy lands. Warm clothes,
  fire, hot drinks and the right armor protect you.
  Blood moons bring more monsters, and better spoils. Meteor showers leave craters with starmetal at their hearts. Auroras dance in
  cold skies and quicken mana. Wyverns cross the sky on some nights.

TOWNS
  Use a Town Charter where you wish a town to stand. A charter stone is set down and the land around it becomes yours. Use the stone
  to see your town, rename it and call newcomers.
  Folk arrive each day while there are free beds. Each has a calling: blacksmith, jeweler, engineer, scholar, hunter, guard, merchant, architect, farmer, miner, alchemist, mage, innkeeper, bard.
  Speak with them to trade and to ask for their services. The architect will even build for you. The scholar teaches recipes.
  Every town remembers how you treat it. Trade, finish bounties and defend it, and you will be offered better.
  The Hollow Host raids growing towns in three waves. Walls, guards, traps, turrets and alarm bells help.
  Merchant caravans pass between towns, and a Shop Counter lets others buy from you while you are away.
  A town rises in rank as it gains beds, workplaces, defenses and shops within its borders:
  Camp: where every town begins.
  Hamlet: 3 beds, 1 workplaces.
  Village: 6 beds, 3 workplaces.
  Town: 12 beds, 6 workplaces, 2 defenses.
  City: 20 beds, 10 workplaces, 4 defenses, 2 shops.
  Kingdom: 32 beds, 16 workplaces, 6 defenses, 3 shops.

DEFENSE
  Spike Trap: hurts hostile things that step on it.
  Flame Vent: sets them alight.
  Alarm Bell: rings when danger comes within 14 blocks, and tells everyone nearby where.
  Ward Stone: nothing hostile is born within 28 blocks of it.
  Reinforced blocks are harder to break. Arrow Turrets and Ward Pylons need flux.

BLUEPRINTS
  Copy an area:
    Sneak and use the Genesis Wand on one corner block, then on the opposite corner.
    Or look at a block and type /build corner 1, then look at the other corner and type /build corner 2.
    Then /build copy myhouse. Add the word air to also copy empty space, so pasting clears room.
  Paste it:
    /build paste myhouse places it in front of you, facing the way you face.
    Add rotate 90, rotate 180, rotate 270 (or left, right, around).
    Add exact to line it up with north like the original, with its corner at your feet.
    Add here to start right where you stand, up 5 or down 3 to move it, at X Y Z for exact coordinates.
    Add clear to also place the air, instant to place it all at once, slow to watch it go up.
  Build it yourself with the overlay (like Litematica):
    /build overlay myhouse. Green sparkles show where blocks go, red dust marks wrong blocks.
    A soft note plays at the closest missing block, so you can find it by sound.
    The bar at the bottom of the screen says the next block, which way it faces, and how far ahead, left or right, up or down.
    /build next says the same thing in chat and plays a chime at that spot.
    /build check gives your progress in percent and lists what you still need.
    /build fill layer places the rest of the layer you're on. /build overlay off stops.
    Add quiet to turn off the notes. Overlay takes the same rotate, exact and here words as paste.
  Materials:
    /build materials myhouse lists every item you need, with stack counts.
    /build supplies myhouse puts all of it in chests behind you.
  Others: /build blueprints lists them, /build blueprint info myhouse, /build blueprint delete myhouse.
  Copies are saved in the world. The book has a Blueprints and Pictures page with all of this as buttons.

GENESIS STUDIO (pictures, 3D models and schematics)
  Genesis Studio is a web page. Open Genesis_Studio.html (it works offline in Chrome or Edge). It has three steps: get Genesis, turn anything into blocks, send it to Minecraft.
  3D models: OBJ (pick its .mtl and textures too), STL, GLB, glTF and MagicaVoxel VOX. Pick the size of the longest side, hollow or solid, and the model colors or one color.
  Picture to blocks:
    Choose a picture, pick Wall (stands up), Floor (lies flat, like map art) or 3D relief (brighter parts come toward you).
    Pick the width in blocks, from 4 to 512. Pick which kinds of blocks to use.
    Color blending: Crisp for pixel art, Smooth for photos. Turn on Pixel art source for sharp pixel art.
    Press Make blueprint. It tells you the size, how many blocks, and the most used blocks.
  Import a schematic: .litematic (Litematica), .schem (WorldEdit), .nbt (Java structure blocks), .mcstructure (Bedrock).
    Java block names are changed to Bedrock ones, and stairs, doors, slabs, logs, beds, torches and lanterns keep their direction.
  Your library: every blueprint you make goes here. Material list and Save list as text for each one.
    Name the library, then press Save library pack. Open the .mcpack file and Minecraft imports it.
    In your world settings, under Behavior Packs, turn on Genesis Library next to Genesis.
    When the world loads, Genesis tells you how many blueprints it got. Then paste, overlay or list materials as above.
    Saving again with the same library name updates the same pack.
  If the Studio hands you a .zip instead, rename it to end in .mcpack.

WHAT GENESIS STUDIO CAN READ
  Genesis Studio looks inside a file to learn what it is, so even a file with the wrong name or no name at all is understood.
  Pictures and photos: PNG, JPG, GIF, WebP, BMP, ICO, SVG, AVIF, TIFF, Photoshop PSD, Targa TGA, QOI and Netpbm. HEIC photos open where the browser allows it.
  3D models: OBJ with its materials and textures, STL, GLB, glTF, PLY with colors, OFF, 3MF, Collada DAE, Blockbench bbmodel, Bedrock geometry and MagicaVoxel VOX.
  Schematics: Litematica, WorldEdit schem, old MCEdit schematic files from before 1.13, Java structure nbt and Bedrock mcstructure, and mcfunction files full of setblock and fill commands.
  Videos: MP4, WebM, MOV and more give you five moments to choose from. Music and sounds: MP3, WAV, OGG, FLAC and others become a picture of how they sound. MIDI music becomes a piano roll.
  Documents: every page of a PDF. Word and PowerPoint files give up their pictures and their writing. Spreadsheets and CSV tables become bar charts. Plain writing becomes a sign, and Studio offers it to you as a /build description too.
  Archives: zip, tar and gzip are opened and searched, and so are add on packs, so a pack full of structures lists every one. Fonts become a sample sign.
  Anything else becomes a pattern of its own bytes in color, because every file has a shape.
  When a file holds many things, Studio lists them all and lets you choose.


SCULPTURES FROM PICTURES
  In Genesis Studio, choose a picture, then choose Sculpture under How it stands. The Studio learns the background from the edges of
  the picture, finds the subject that stands apart from it, and rounds that outline into a full statue: thick where the subject is broad,
  thin where it is slender, with light and shadow pressed into the surface. Choose how thick (slim, natural or round), how much surface
  detail, and hollow or solid. It works best with one subject on a plain or softly lit background, like a statue, a figure or a creature.
  Leave out the background works for flat pictures too.

SPEAKING IN OTHER TONGUES
  Speak to me in your own tongue and I will understand. /build un château bleu, /build 大きな赤い城, /build ein großes Haus mit Garten.
  Japanese, Chinese and Korean need no spaces. Choose a language and I will also speak it in the Codex and in my most common words.
  /build words tells you how many words I know.
